Two evaluation traps that make random noise look like progress

Before claiming any weight edit works, we ran norm-matched Gaussian noise and transcript audits on Qwen3-0.6B. Both controls exposed failure modes that invalidate naive greedy and boxed-answer math benchmarks.

Watch the illusions collapse under strict auditing

On greedy MATH-500 (temp = 0.0), six zero-information draws span a 6.0 pp chaotic band because 14 of 14 rescued problems were baseline 2048-token timeouts getting randomly rerolled. Under official sampling (temp = 0.6, N = 300), random noise falls to -0.33 pp (p = 1.000).

The Greedy-Perturbation Illusion

When a small reasoning model hits its token budget on more than half of problems, almost any small weight perturbation flips an early argmax token and acts as an unguided trajectory reroll. Truncated failures gain a nonzero chance to finish, while short correct traces remain stable. That asymmetry gives pure zero-information Gaussian noise a fake +6.0 pp boost at temp = 0.0 with zero loop reduction (29 to 30 loops, p = 1.000).

The Final-Answer Echo Trap

Standard math scripts match the last boxed string anywhere in the reply. High-dose BA-WSC (alpha = 0.70) reached 44.0% standard accuracy (p = 0.0294) at -0.022 perplexity delta, yet raw transcripts revealed the edit kept firing after closing the think tag, echoing the final answer until cutoff and more than doubling loops (29 to 59, p = 0.0001). Under Strict Clean-EOS (correct, closed think tag, natural stop), it plunged to 32.0%, worse than random noise (p = 0.0106).

Minimum protocol for future weight-editing papers

Every experiment in this repository enforces Frobenius-matched Gaussian noise controls, multi-seed stochastic sampling (temp = 0.6), Strict Clean-EOS grading, and anti-leakage calibration, dev, and held-out splits committed before test evaluation.

Virtual Constant-1 Bias Register (b*)

Modern SwiGLU MLPs omit explicit bias vectors, preventing direct affine thresholds. Because post-RMSNorm activations keep stable anisotropic mean structure across domains, a closed-form ridge vector b* satisfies b* dot x close to 1 out-of-sample (std = 0.020 to 0.074 across all three architectures), enabling clean bias-free threshold gates with zero architectural modifications.

Null-Space-Gated Oblique Surgery (oblique_res)

Solves a rank-one update in W_down whose left detector lies orthogonal to the top-64 preservation subspace of normal text and copying, steering stalled activations toward clean think-tag resolution. Cuts WikiText-2 perplexity penalty nearly tenfold over unconditional ablation (+0.141 vs +1.338) and ranks first on 3 of 3 architectures at 8-bit (+6.00 pp pooled, p = 0.000130, 20% to 30% faster).

Nonlinear Dormant SwiGLU Compilation (BA-WSC)

Compiles an IF-THEN threshold gate directly into 32 (or 256) lowest-RMS dormant SwiGLU neurons via b* and gauge factor eta. Because SiLU suppresses negative pre-activations exponentially, normal text sees nearly zero perplexity drift (-0.018 to +0.028 across all three models) while significantly raising think-tag closure across every family.

Zero-shot transfer proves metacognitive stopping, not math memorization

Calibrated strictly on math, exact-copy, and WikiText-2 with zero science questions anywhere in calibration or development, the exact same frozen Qwen3-0.6B edits generalize to out-of-distribution GSM8K-100 (+9.0 pp vs baseline p = 0.049, +16.0 pp vs random noise p = 0.0001, 30% faster wall time) and zero-shot ARC-Challenge science (+15.0 pp for oblique surgery p = 0.0003; +11.0 pp for BA-WSC p = 0.0074), while preserving Copy-90 retrieval intact.

Why four-bit quantization shreds weight edits, and the zero-byte fix

Quantizing small reasoning models to Q4_0 or Q3_K_M triggers 2.3x to 8.2x repetition-loop explosions. Worse, standard floating-point weight edits break inside GGUF block quantization because edited weights share FP16 block scales with unedited neighbors. Permuting dormant SwiGLU neurons into a dedicated hardware block fixes this structurally at zero byte and latency cost.

Behavioral proof across two independent architectures

Block alignment is not just a tensor-norm metric. When we deploy the exact same frozen edits from 8-bit down to 4-bit and 3-bit, diffuse oblique surgery degrades as quantization collateral builds, while block-aligned BA-WSC holds steady and takes first place.

On hybrid Qwen3.5-0.8B (Q8_0 to Q4_0), diffuse oblique drops from 10 to 7 solved while BA-WSC climbs from 3 to 15 (exact permutation crossover p = 0.00336). On DeepSeek-R1-1.5B (Q8_0 to Q4_0 to Q3_K_M), BA-WSC holds a flat +6, +6, +6 advantage over baseline across every bit-width while diffuse oblique slides from +10 to +4 to -3. Combined two-model Fisher crossover significance reaches p = 0.00210 (Clean-EOS) and p = 0.00530 (standard).

Compiling directly on the dequantized low-bit model itself (Post-Quantization Weight-Space Healing, PQ-BA-WSC) beats floating-point transfer at both bit-widths on Qwen3-0.6B, healing 83% of the 4-bit cliff with oblique surgery (p = 0.0041) and ranking first overall at 3-bit Q3_K_M (+68% relative gain, p = 0.0044, 19x less perplexity damage than unconditional WSP).

Exact 100,000-permutation interaction swing: +15 of 150 items (+10.00 pp), two-sided p = 0.00336. Combined Fisher meta-analysis with DeepSeek-R1-1.5B: chi-squared = 16.82, p = 0.00210.

Honest limitations and retained negative results

All experiments ran on 0.6B to 1.5B local models using one AMD Radeon RX 6400 (4 GB VRAM, Vulkan) with zero paid compute. Models 2 and 3 used single-seed N = 150 rollouts per bit-width; scaling to 7B to 32B models is straightforward future work since every solver is closed-form. At 8-bit Q8_0, oblique surgery leads raw accuracy while BA-WSC wins on zero perplexity drift and low-bit survival.
